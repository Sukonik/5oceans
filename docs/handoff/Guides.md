# Guides
## Start with the decision
A visitor can start with a place or with a retirement question. Both routes should produce a monthly budget, a timeline of accessible income, a shortfall or surplus, and one next action with an official source. Show the plan subtitle beside the animal name for people who have never seen FiveOceans.

| Starting point | Collect first | Compare | Send the visitor to |
|---|---|---|---|
| I want Fridays off at 45 | Actual take-home pay at each schedule, employer coverage threshold, pension service impact | Same employer at four days vs. leaving for another job | Electric Eel, Pink River Dolphin, Yeti Crab |
| I want a home that earns rent | Intended occupancy, lawful units, available equity, verified long-term rent | Buy a duplex, build an ADU, or keep the present home | Hermit Crab, Royal Tern |
| I have no pension | Existing workplace plans, IRA, taxable savings, anticipated Social Security | Projected drawdown before and after Social Security | Sea Pig, Bobcat, Colossal Squid |
| I have several old jobs | Employers and dates, prior plan statements, tracing leads | Known benefits vs. possible benefits awaiting confirmation | Colossal Squid and the DOL Retirement Savings Lost and Found |

### Starting at 35
Use fictional inputs in public examples. Ask for monthly spending including healthcare, spendable assets today, an affordable contribution, the month work changes, and the months each benefit starts. Keep pretax balances and spendable equivalents clearly labeled. A dollar already counted in a 457(b) cannot appear again as brokerage cash.

**Prototype regression example.** Start at zero. Deposit $600 at each month end from 35 to 45. At a constant 3% annual real return the balance is approximately $83,669. An immediate monthly draw across the next 18 years at 2% annual real return supports about $460 a month. A $1,000 monthly gap calls for roughly $181,847 at 45. These use effective monthly rates and inflation-adjusted dollars. Health and tax assumptions belong in the inputs. This calculation stops at the first pension date; model the interval to Social Security and the later-life budget separately.

## Paycheck decisions
Write down each paycheck’s actual net amount and pay frequency. A biweekly employee usually receives 26 checks per year; two checks per month gives 24. Calculate the annual total first. For Alligator, compare two uses of the same spare dollars with the same debt APR assumptions, minimum payments, match formula and vesting schedule. Public employees may already make mandatory pension contributions. Ask separately about voluntary accounts and any actual employer match.

**Missed opportunity to surface.** A visitor with several prior private-sector jobs can search the federal retirement-plan database and contact the old plan administrators. Results are leads to verify. Public-sector and IRA accounts require other searches. [DOL Retirement Savings Lost and Found](https://lostandfound.dol.gov/)

## Family and care
For the Sea Lion or Scarlet Ibis comparison, price rent or ownership, trips home, expected hours of unpaid help, paid replacement care and transport. Assign a dollar value to unpaid help only when the visitor elects to model paying for a substitute. Print two totals so the family sees cash spending separately from the value of time. Find the local Area Agency on Aging and ask what services, wait lists and residency rules apply. [Eldercare Locator](https://eldercare.acl.gov/)

## Visitor handoff
Each result should say “Next action” followed by a real step, such as “Ask HR for the first day coverage ends if you move to three days.” Include source owner, jurisdiction, review date and whether the input came from a statement, quote or estimate. Offer a generic route when a visitor skips a personal question. Use public, synthetic data for demos. Keep account numbers, exact addresses, tax forms and uploaded statements out of the public repository.

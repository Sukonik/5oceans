## What it is
A governmental 457(b) is a workplace savings plan offered by state and local governments, such as New York State, a city, a county or a school district. Contributions come out of your paycheck before income tax (many plans also offer a Roth option), grow tax-deferred, and are taxed as ordinary income when withdrawn.

## Why it matters for leaving work early
Most retirement accounts charge an additional 10% tax on withdrawals before age 59½. A governmental 457(b) is different: money you contributed to the plan itself (often called native funds) generally escapes that additional tax when you take it after you separate from the employer. Ordinary income tax still applies to pretax withdrawals. That is why this plan anchors the early-retirement bridge in [Yeti Crab](animal:yeti-crab).

> Rolling a 457(b) into an IRA can change this. Money in an IRA is generally subject to the early-withdrawal rules for IRAs. Think before you roll over if you may need the money before 59½.

## Contribution limits for 2026
- **Regular limit:** $24,500 of your own deferrals.
- **Age 50 and over:** a catch-up of $8,000 is generally available if your plan permits it, for up to $32,500.
- **Ages 60 to 63:** a higher catch-up of $11,250 applies in 2026 in plans that allow it.
- **Special 457(b) catch-up:** a plan may let you contribute extra in the three years before your plan's normal retirement age. You can't combine it with the age-50 catch-up.
- **Separate limit:** the 457(b) limit is separate from the 401(k) or 403(b) limit, so an employee offered both may be able to defer in each.

## When can you take the money out
Typically after you separate from the employer, in an unforeseeable emergency as the plan defines it, or at a later age. Some plans also allow in-service withdrawals from age 59½. Plans differ, so read your plan document and ask the administrator.

## Same employer, fewer hours
Reducing your hours is not the same as separating. If you stay with the same employer at four days, the plan may treat you as still employed and keep the money locked. That is the scenario to test in [Electric Eel](animal:electric-eel) and [Dumbo Octopus](animal:dumbo-octopus) before you count on the balance.

## Questions to ask your administrator
- Is this plan governmental? (A 457(b) at a nonprofit is a different, less flexible kind.)
- What counts as separation from service, and how soon can I withdraw?
- What portion of my balance came from other plans through rollovers?
- Can I set up a recurring monthly payment, and can I change or stop it?
- Does the plan offer a Roth option, and what are the fees and fund choices?

## Mistakes to avoid
- Counting the balance as spendable before you know the plan allows a withdrawal.
- Rolling to an IRA and losing the early-access advantage without meaning to.
- Forgetting income tax: a $2,000 net draw needs a larger gross withdrawal.
- Ignoring required minimum distributions later in life. See [Traditional IRA](resource:traditional-ira) for the general rules.

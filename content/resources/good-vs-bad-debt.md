## A simple test
For each debt, find its **effective rate**: the interest rate, reduced if the interest is tax-deductible. Compare it with the return you can reasonably expect after fees and tax.

- **Bad debt:** costs more than you can expect to earn. A 22% card balance is the clearest case. Paying it off is a guaranteed 22% return, better than any normal investment.
- **Good debt:** cheap and tied to something that holds value or earns, such as a home or a rental at a low fixed rate, and the payment fits easily in your budget.
- **Judgment call:** rates close to your expected return, or debt that does not buy an asset, such as many student and car loans. Risk and peace of mind matter.

## What to do first
1. **Take any employer match.** It is an immediate return.
2. **Build an emergency fund.** Without one, a surprise bill lands on a card.
3. **Pay down high-rate debt.** Highest rate first saves the most interest.
4. **Then invest** while keeping cheap, safe debt at its normal payments.

## Why returns are not like rates
A debt rate is certain. An investment return is not. If markets fall while you owe money at a high rate, you lose twice. Use a modest return in your plan. A lower assumed return makes paying debt look better.

## Using debt well
- A fixed-rate mortgage on a home you can afford can be a way to own an asset, and it can be cheaper than renting. See [Primary home](resource:primary-home) and [Duplex](resource:duplex-house-hacking).
- A rental or business loan is good only if the income covers the payment with room to spare. Test a bad year in the [Real Estate Calculator](page:tools/real-estate/index.html).
- Do not borrow to invest unless you could carry the loan through a long downturn.

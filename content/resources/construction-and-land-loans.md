## Land loans
Lenders treat raw land as riskier than a finished home, so a land loan often needs a **larger down payment**, a **higher rate** and a **shorter term** than a mortgage. The land's price, access, utilities and zoning all matter to the lender. If you already own land, its equity may count toward your construction down payment.

## Construction loans
A construction loan pays for the building in **draws** as work reaches stages, with an inspection before each. You usually pay **interest only** on the amount drawn during construction.

- **Construction-to-permanent (one-time close):** one closing, and the loan converts to a regular mortgage when the home is finished. You lock the terms up front and close once.
- **Construction-only (two-time close):** a short-term loan that you refinance into a mortgage after completion, with a second set of closing costs and a second approval.

## What lenders ask for
- Plans, a budget and a signed contract with a licensed builder
- Permits and proof of zoning compliance
- A contingency reserve
- Your credit, income and reserves, as for any mortgage
- An appraisal of the home as it will be

## Risks to price
- **Delays:** each extra month adds interest and the carrying cost of the land. Six extra months at $1,400 is $8,400.
- **Overruns:** a change order or a price jump can exceed the budget.
- **Flood and site issues:** check the flood zone, septic and wetlands before buying.

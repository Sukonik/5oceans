## Free reports
You can check your credit report at each of the three nationwide bureaus once a week, free, at AnnualCreditReport.com. This is the only site authorized by federal law to fill these requests. Other sites may charge you or collect your information for other purposes.

## Look for
- Accounts you do not recognize, which may be identity theft.
- Late payments you made on time.
- Balances or limits that are wrong.
- Old negative items that should have aged off.
- Differences between the three bureaus. Each can hold different data.

## Dispute an error
Tell the bureau and the company that reported the information. Explain what is wrong, attach proof, and keep copies. The bureau generally must investigate and respond. Follow the CFPB's steps. Be cautious of anyone who charges to "fix" your credit or tells you to dispute accurate information. Regulators have warned about credit-repair promises and online "tricks".

## Credit freezes
A freeze stops new creditors from seeing your report, which makes it hard for a thief to open credit in your name. Under federal law it is free at all three bureaus. Ask each bureau separately; one does not notify the others. A freeze should be placed within one business day when you ask online or by phone and lifted quickly when you want to apply for credit. Paid "credit locks" are not required: the CFPB says a freeze is the free option.

## Fraud alerts
A fraud alert asks lenders to verify your identity before opening credit. It is also free. Use a freeze if you want the stronger protection.

## When to check
- Before applying for a mortgage or car loan.
- After a data breach notice.
- At least once or twice a year as a habit.

## Keep scores in view
Reports do not usually include a score. Record your scores in the [Credit Card Tool](page:credit/index.html) after each check. See [Credit scores](resource:credit-scores).

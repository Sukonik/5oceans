## The four dates to collect
Before you leave work, write down: the **last day worked**, the **actual end of employer coverage**, the **earliest start of replacement coverage**, and the **first pension deposit**. Confirm dependent coverage and any employer retiree-health rules in writing.

## If you leave before 65
- **COBRA:** where it applies, confirm the notice, premium, election deadline, coverage period and any retroactive premium due.
- **Marketplace:** compare plans for your expected annual income. In New York use NY State of Health. Consider the network, deductible and prescriptions, not only the premium.
- **Retiree coverage:** some public employers offer it. Check eligibility and cost before choosing a retirement date.

## Medicare at 65
- Your **Initial Enrollment Period** lasts seven months. It starts three months before the month you turn 65 and ends three months after.
- If you miss it, you may have to wait for another enrollment period and pay a monthly late enrollment penalty for as long as you have Part B. The General Enrollment Period runs January 1 through March 31, with coverage starting the month after you sign up.
- Part A is premium-free for most people with enough work credits. Coverage always starts on the first of a month.

> COBRA and retiree coverage generally do not count as a reason to delay Part B without a penalty, the way coverage from a current employer can. Check with Medicare before you rely on it.

## What Medicare does not do
- Medicare generally covers care outside the US only in limited situations. If you plan to live abroad part of the year, price separate coverage. See [Giant Manta Ray](animal:giant-manta-ray).
- It generally does not cover most long-term custodial care. Compare paid help, family capacity and state programs. See [Swan](animal:swan).

## Help with costs
Medicare Savings Programs can help people with limited income and resources pay premiums or cost-sharing. A State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) counselor can explain your choices at no charge.

## Medical savings
If you are in a qualifying high-deductible plan, an [HSA](resource:hsa) can pay for medical costs. You generally cannot contribute once you are enrolled in Medicare.

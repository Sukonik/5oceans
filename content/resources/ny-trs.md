## What it is
New York has several public pension systems. Teachers and certain administrators outside New York City are generally covered by the **New York State Teachers' Retirement System (NYSTRS)**. New York City teachers are covered by the **Teachers' Retirement System of the City of New York (TRS NYC)**. Other state and local employees are covered by [ERS or PFRS](resource:ny-ers).

Do not assume the rules match across systems. Vesting, early-retirement ages, reductions and the formula depend on the system, your tier and your plan.

## What to collect before you decide
- Your **tier** and plan, from your member statement or member portal.
- Credited **service**, including any prior service you could purchase.
- **Final average salary** as the system defines it.
- **Official benefit estimates** at your earliest eligible age, at full retirement age, and at the date you would stop working.
- Rules for **part-time** or reduced schedules and for returning to work after retiring.
- **Retiree health insurance** eligibility, which is set by the district or employer, not by the pension system.

## Compare two scenarios
As with ERS, ask for one estimate assuming you keep working and one with service and salary frozen at departure. Compare the pension, the health coverage and the savings available to bridge the gap. [Pink River Dolphin](animal:pink-river-dolphin) walks through this comparison.

## Extra savings
Teachers often have access to a **403(b)** and sometimes a [governmental 457(b)](resource:governmental-457b) through the district. The 403(b) follows the [401(k)/403(b)](resource:401k-403b) rules. Ask whether your district offers a 457(b) and who administers it.

## Social Security
Some New York public employees do not pay into Social Security through their public job. Check your earnings record at the Social Security Administration before you plan around a benefit. The Windfall Elimination Provision and Government Pension Offset, which used to reduce benefits for some public employees, were repealed by the Social Security Fairness Act in January 2025, so older advice that mentions them may be out of date. See [Social Security](resource:social-security).

## What they are
A 401(k) is offered by many private employers. A 403(b) is offered by public schools, universities, hospitals and many nonprofits. Both let you save from your paycheck, often with an employer match, either before tax (traditional) or after tax (Roth) if the plan offers it.

## 2026 limits
The employee limit for 401(k), 403(b), governmental 457(b) and the federal Thrift Savings Plan is **$24,500**. Participants age 50 or older can generally add **$8,000**, for up to $32,500. In plans that allow it, ages 60 through 63 can add **$11,250** instead of $8,000.

## Getting the money out
- **Age 59½ or later:** withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income for pretax money, with no additional 10% tax.
- **Before 59½:** an additional 10% tax usually applies unless an exception fits, such as separation from service in or after the year you turn 55 (the "rule of 55"), disability, or substantially equal periodic payments.
- **Rolling over:** you can roll the balance to an IRA or a new employer's plan. The rule of 55 does not carry over to an IRA, so do not roll the money first if you plan to use it.
- **Required withdrawals:** minimum distributions begin later in life under federal law. Confirm the current starting age for your birth year.

## Employer match
A match is part of pay. Contribute at least enough to get the full match if you can. Ask about the vesting schedule, because unvested match can disappear if you leave.

## Compare with a governmental 457(b)
If you work for a government and have both, the 457(b) usually offers better access before 59½. See [Governmental 457(b)](resource:governmental-457b).

## Questions to ask
- What is the match and when does it vest?
- Does the plan offer a Roth option and in-plan conversions?
- What are the investment fees?
- Does the plan allow partial withdrawals after I leave, or does it require a full cash-out?

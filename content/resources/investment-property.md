## What it is
An investment property is a house or unit bought primarily to rent out. It is a business as well as a purchase: you carry the property through empty months, repairs and tenant turnover.

## A worked example (fictional)
An owner-occupied duplex costs **$3,300 a month** to carry (mortgage $2,100, property tax $600, insurance $180, flood $100, common utilities $120, maintenance and reserve $200). The other unit rents for **$2,000**.
- At **95% economic occupancy**, rent collected is $1,900.
- **Management at 8%** of collected rent is $152.
- Rent available toward carrying costs is **$1,748**, so the owner pays **$1,552 a month** before income tax.
- To cover the full $3,300, gross monthly rent would need to be about **$3,776**.

**Bad year:** at 75% occupancy the offset falls to $1,380, so the owner pays $1,920 a month. Add a $6,000 uninsured loss and the year costs **$29,040**, or about $2,420 a month. Always test the bad year.

## Costs to include
Purchase and closing costs, debt service, property tax, landlord insurance, repairs, capital reserves, vacancy, management, advertising, legal, and utilities you pay. Investor loans usually carry different terms than a loan for a primary home, so use a lender quote.

## Taxes
Rental income is taxable. You can deduct ordinary and necessary expenses such as mortgage interest, taxes, insurance, maintenance and depreciation, generally reported on Schedule E. Principal payments and reserve deposits are not current deductions. Depreciation can reduce taxable income without a cash outflow, but when you sell, depreciation previously allowed can be taxed. Land is not depreciable. See Publication 527 and Topic 414.

## Selling
A like-kind exchange can defer gain if you reinvest in qualifying real property under strict rules. Without it, subtract loan payoff, selling costs and tax before treating any proceeds as retirement cash.

## Insurance and tenants
Use landlord cover, not an owner-only policy. Confirm vacancy clauses and loss-of-rent coverage. Check local landlord–tenant rules on leases, deposits and notices. [Insurance](Insurance.md#recreation-and-liability) in the reference guides covers these questions.

## Compare with simpler options
Compare the same starting equity in a rental and in a diversified portfolio: spendable cash, time required, concentration, liquidity and downside. Count cash flow, principal paydown and appreciation separately. See [Primary home](resource:primary-home) and [Duplex](resource:duplex-house-hacking).

## What it is
A one-participant 401(k) is a 401(k) for a business owner with no employees other than a spouse. You can contribute as the **employee** (up to the employee deferral limit, $24,500 for 2026 with catch-up if you are 50 or older) and as the **employer** (a profit-sharing contribution based on net self-employment earnings), subject to the overall annual limit, which is $72,000 for 2026 before catch-up contributions.

## Who it fits
[Black Seadevil](animal:black-seadevil) is the side-income route: a visitor with net self-employment earnings who wants to save more than an IRA allows. Check the employee census, net earned income and any workplace deferrals at your day job, because employee deferrals are shared across plans.

## Rules to verify
- **No employees** other than you and your spouse, apart from certain part-time or excluded workers. Hiring changes the plan.
- Employee deferrals count toward the **same limit** as your 401(k) or 403(b) at another employer.
- Plans over a certain size require an annual IRS filing.
- Set the plan up before the deadline for the tax year in which you want to contribute.

## Budget for taxes first
Calculate spendable profit after business expenses, income tax and self-employment tax. Make estimated tax payments as needed. A contribution reduces taxable income but does not reduce the self-employment tax base.

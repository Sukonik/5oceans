## Two ways to attack debt
Both start the same way: pay at least the minimum on every debt, every month. Then pick one debt to receive all the extra money. When it is paid off, roll its payment into the next.

- **Avalanche:** target the highest interest rate first. This costs the least in total interest.
- **Snowball:** target the smallest balance first. You may pay more interest, but early wins can help you stick with the plan.

Choose the one you will actually follow.

## Make the extra money
Cut one regular expense, sell something you do not need, or direct a raise or tax refund to the target debt. Even a small extra payment shortens the plan.

## Be careful with shortcuts
- **Balance transfers** can lower interest for a while, but they usually carry a fee and a promotional period that ends. Do not run the old card up again.
- **Consolidation loans** can lower your rate, but a longer term can cost more overall.
- **Debt settlement and credit repair companies** often charge high fees and make promises they cannot keep. Be wary.

## Get free help
Nonprofit credit counseling agencies can review your budget and, where it makes sense, set up a debt management plan. Ask about fees and read the terms before agreeing.

## Protect your credit while you pay
Late payments hurt scores the most. Keep autopay on for minimums. Keep old accounts open when you can. See [Credit cards and utilization](resource:credit-cards-and-utilization).

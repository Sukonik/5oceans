## What it is
A second or vacation home is a dwelling you own in addition to your main home, used for part of the year and sometimes rented to others. [Royal Tern](animal:royal-tern) models months at each home with permitted rental months while away. [Giant Squid](animal:giant-squid) compares holding two homes with selling one.

## The IRS day-count test
For tax purposes, count three kinds of days: **days rented at a fair price**, **personal-use days** and **vacant days**.
- You are treated as **using the home as a residence** if your personal days exceed the greater of **14 days** or **10% of the days rented** at fair rental value. When you do, rental expenses are limited and you cannot deduct a rental loss.
- If you use the home as a residence and rent it for **fewer than 15 days**, you do not report the rental income and you do not deduct rental expenses.
- Keep a calendar. The calendar is your evidence.

## Costs to price
Mortgage or cash opportunity cost, property tax, insurance (a second-home policy often differs from a primary-home policy), HOA dues, utilities while empty, maintenance, travel, and flood insurance if applicable. Ask lenders how they treat second-home financing, because down payments and rates often differ from a primary residence.

## Rental use
Verify local zoning, minimum rental terms, registration, taxes, HOA and mortgage restrictions before counting any rental revenue. A change in use can change the insurance policy: confirm cover for long-term versus short-term tenants with a licensed insurer. See the [Investment property](resource:investment-property) page for landlord costs.

## New York residency
New York taxes residents on all income. You can be a **statutory resident** if you maintain a permanent place of abode in New York and spend 184 or more days there, whether or not New York is your domicile. A day count alone does not settle domicile. If you live part of the year elsewhere, such as a Caribbean winter, read the tax department's definitions and keep records of where you spend each day.

## Questions to ask
- How many personal-use and rental days do I expect each year?
- Do HOA or zoning rules allow my planned rental use?
- What does the insurer require when the home is vacant?
- Is it worth holding two homes, or would renting the second place for the season cost less?

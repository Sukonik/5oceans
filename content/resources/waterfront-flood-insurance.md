## The basics
- **Homeowners policies generally exclude flood.** Flood insurance is separate.
- The **National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP)** offers policies for homeowners, renters and businesses. For a single-family home, building coverage is available up to **$250,000** and contents coverage up to **$100,000**.
- There is usually a **30-day waiting period** before an NFIP policy takes effect, with exceptions such as a policy bought with a loan closing. Do not wait for a storm warning.
- Standard NFIP policies generally exclude additional living expenses and lost rent. Price displacement and rental interruption separately.
- Premiums are unique to your property and depend on several factors. Some home changes can lower the cost.

## What to ask for at each address
Request separate figures for:
- Home or condo coverage
- Wind or named-storm deductible (fixed amount or a percentage, and of what)
- Flood building and contents coverage
- Exclusions, such as basements and accessory structures
- Temporary living costs

Ask about condo master policy versus an individual policy. A flood map, a broker's indication and a policy declaration each answer a different question, so use a dated quote.

## Build the stress test
- **Displacement:** nights out, reasonable nightly cost, stored contents and interrupted rent.
- **Deductible:** include once in the shock scenario.
- **Reserve:** keep the storm reserve in an insured account and out of the retirement bridge. [Red Cushion Sea Star](animal:red-cushion-sea-star) tests access.

Count an insurance recovery only after you have a written quote and a documented payable benefit. Use zero for unverified reimbursement in the stress case. [Goblin Shark](animal:goblin-shark) runs a market fall plus six months of displacement.

## Check the map
Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center to see the flood zone for an address. A map is only a starting point: ask about history of flooding on the access road.

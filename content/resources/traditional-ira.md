## What it is
A traditional IRA is an individual retirement account. Contributions may be fully or partly deductible, depending on your filing status, income and whether you or your spouse are covered by a retirement plan at work. Earnings are not taxed until you take a distribution.

## 2026 limits
The combined limit across all your traditional and Roth IRAs is **$7,500**, or **$8,600** if you are 50 or older, or your taxable compensation if less.

If you or your spouse are covered by a workplace plan, the deduction phases out at these 2026 income ranges:
- **Single, covered at work:** $81,000 to $91,000.
- **Married filing jointly, contributor covered at work:** $129,000 to $149,000.
- **Married filing jointly, contributor not covered but spouse is:** $242,000 to $252,000.

If neither of you is covered at work, the phase-outs do not apply.

## Rollovers
Old workplace balances are often rolled into a traditional IRA to simplify investments. A direct rollover (trustee to trustee) avoids withholding and tax. Before rolling, check whether the old plan has a feature you would lose, such as the early access of a [governmental 457(b)](resource:governmental-457b) or the rule of 55 in a [401(k)](resource:401k-403b).

## Taking money out
Withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income, apart from any after-tax basis. Before 59½, an additional 10% tax applies unless an exception fits. Required minimum distributions begin later in life under federal rules. Confirm the starting age for your birth year with the IRS.

## Traditional or Roth
Pick traditional when the deduction is valuable now and you expect a lower tax rate later. Pick [Roth](resource:roth-ira) when you want tax-free withdrawals and flexibility. Many people use both. The annual limit is shared.

## What they are
A conventional loan is not backed by a government agency. Most are "conforming" loans that meet the rules and loan limits of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, which buy them from lenders. Larger loans are "jumbo" loans with each lender's own rules.

## 2026 loan limits
FHFA set the 2026 baseline limit for a one-unit home at **$832,750**, up from $806,500 in 2025. In high-cost areas the limit goes up to **$1,249,125**. Limits are higher for two to four units. Check your county's limit with FHFA or a lender.

## Down payment and mortgage insurance
- **20% down** avoids private mortgage insurance (PMI).
- **Less than 20% down** usually means PMI. Some programs for first-time or lower-income buyers, such as Fannie Mae's HomeReady, allow as little as 3% down. Confirm the current terms and income limits with a lender.
- **PMI can usually be removed.** Under federal law you can generally ask to cancel PMI when your balance reaches 80% of the home's original value, and it ends automatically at 78% if you are current. Ask your lender for the details.

## Credit
Lenders set their own minimums. Many look for a 620 score or higher, and better scores earn better rates. Check all three of your scores in the [Credit Card Tool](page:credit/index.html).

## Who it fits
Buyers with good credit and stable income, especially with 5% to 20% down. A second home or investment property is usually financed conventionally, with a larger down payment and higher rate than a primary home.

## Compare with the alternatives
- [FHA loans](resource:fha-loans) when credit is lower or savings are thin.
- [VA loans](resource:va-loans) for eligible veterans and service members.
- [USDA loans](resource:usda-loans) for eligible rural areas.

## What a credit score is
A credit score estimates how likely you are to repay a loan on time. It is calculated from the information in your credit report, not from your income, job or savings. Scores typically run from 300 to 850.

## You have more than one
Equifax, Experian and TransUnion are separate companies. Each may hold slightly different information about you, and several scoring models (FICO 8, FICO 9, VantageScore 3.0 and 4.0, and others) turn that information into numbers. So you can have many valid scores at once. When you compare, compare like with like: the same model, from the same bureau, over time.

## What goes into a FICO score
FICO says its score is built from five parts, with approximate weights:
- **Payment history (about 35%).** Paying on time is the strongest signal. Late payments hurt more when they are recent, severe and frequent.
- **Amounts owed (about 30%).** Total debt and how much of your available revolving credit you use, called utilization.
- **Length of credit history (about 15%).** The age of your oldest account, newest account and the average.
- **New credit (about 10%).** Recent applications and new accounts. FICO counts inquiries only from the last 12 months.
- **Credit mix (about 10%).** The kinds of credit you hold and how you pay each.

The weights are general. The exact effect depends on your file.

## How scores tie to housing
Lenders choose their own minimums. For FHA-insured home loans, HUD's current rules allow the largest loan (about 3.5% down) at a score of 580 or above, limit the loan to about 90% of value (about 10% down) from 500 to 579, and do not allow FHA insurance below 500. Mortgage lenders commonly look at the middle of your three scores. See [Primary home](resource:primary-home) and the [Real Estate Calculator](page:tools/real-estate/index.html).

## Reports are not scores
Your free reports show accounts and history. A score is a separate number, and you often pay for it unless a card issuer or bank provides it free. The [Credit Card Tool](page:credit/index.html) lets you record your three scores with the model and date so you can watch the trend.

## Ways to raise a score
1. Pay every bill on time. Autopay for the minimum is a safe floor.
2. Keep card balances low relative to limits and pay before the statement closes.
3. Do not close your oldest card without a reason.
4. Apply for new credit only when you need it.
5. Check each report for errors and dispute them. See [Credit reports and freezes](resource:credit-reports-and-freezes).

## What it is
A Health Savings Account lets you put pretax money aside for health costs if you are covered by a qualifying high-deductible health plan (HDHP). Contributions are deductible, growth is tax-deferred, and withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free.

## 2026 limits (IRS Publication 969)
- **Self-only coverage:** up to $4,400.
- **Family coverage:** up to $8,750.
- **Age 55 or older:** an additional $1,000 catch-up.

## What counts
- **Qualified unreimbursed medical expenses** can be paid tax-free from the account. Keep receipts, and do not claim an expense that insurance already reimbursed.
- **Premiums** qualify only under specific exceptions, such as COBRA or coverage while receiving unemployment benefits.

## Rules to verify
- You must be **eligible** in the months you contribute. Other health coverage, including Medicare, can stop your eligibility to contribute.
- Withdrawals for non-medical purposes are taxed as ordinary income and can carry an additional penalty before age 65. Confirm the current rule in Publication 969.
- Not every employer-offered plan qualifies. Ask HR whether yours is HSA-eligible.

## How it fits an early-retirement plan
An HSA can be a source of tax-free money for medical costs in the years between leaving work and Medicare. [Spider Monkey](animal:spider-monkey) checks eligibility and the timeline. Treat the balance as medical money, not general spending money.

## Keep an eye on
- Investment options and fees in the account.
- Whether you are still eligible to contribute after you change coverage.
- Records of every qualified expense, so you can reimburse yourself later if you choose.

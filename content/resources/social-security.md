## How it works
Social Security retirement benefits are calculated from your indexed earnings. The formula generally uses your **highest 35 years**, so years with no earnings count as zero. If you stop working early, those zeros can lower your benefit.

## When to claim
- **Age 62** is the earliest. For people with a full retirement age of 67, claiming at 62 reduces the monthly benefit to roughly 70% of the full amount, permanently.
- **Full retirement age** is 67 for people born in 1960 or later.
- **Waiting past full retirement age** earns delayed retirement credits until 70.

There is no single right age. Health, other income, marital status and the survivor's needs all matter. Compare household income while both partners are living with the survivor scenario, as [Giant Otter](animal:giant-otter) does.

## Start from your own record
Create or sign in to your my Social Security account and review your earnings history. Errors are easier to correct while records are recent. Ask SSA for an estimate that assumes the work-stop date you are considering, and note which future earnings the estimate assumes. SSA's stop-working planner shows how leaving early can change the amount.

## Survivor benefits
When one spouse dies, the survivor generally keeps the larger of the two benefits. That is a reason for the higher earner to consider delaying. See SSA's survivors page for eligibility details.

## Pairing it with other income
If you retire before you claim, you need money for the gap. Draw from the plan that fits the timing: [457(b)](resource:governmental-457b) after separation, [Roth IRA contributions](resource:roth-ira), or [Treasury bills](resource:treasuries) matched to the months. [Colossal Squid](animal:colossal-squid) puts these on separate timelines.

## Taxes
Some Social Security benefits can be taxable depending on your other income. Plan for it when you size withdrawals.

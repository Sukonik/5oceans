## What they are
FHA loans are made by approved lenders and insured by the Federal Housing Administration. The insurance protects the lender, which is why borrowers with smaller down payments or lower scores can qualify. They are for a home you will live in, including a two-to-four-unit building where you occupy one unit, which makes FHA a common tool for [house hackers](resource:duplex-house-hacking).

## Down payment and score (HUD)
HUD's current rules tie eligibility to the borrower's credit score:
- **580 or higher:** the maximum loan, about **96.5% of the value**, or about 3.5% down.
- **500 to 579:** the loan is limited to about **90% of the value**, or about 10% down.
- **Below 500:** not eligible for FHA insurance.

Lenders may set higher minimums of their own.

## Mortgage insurance
- **Upfront premium:** 1.75% of the base loan. It can be added to the loan.
- **Annual premium:** paid monthly. For a 30-year loan of $726,200 or less with a down payment under 5%, HUD's published rate is 0.55% a year; the rate is lower with a larger down payment. On many loans with a small down payment the premium lasts for the life of the loan, so some borrowers later refinance. Confirm the current table with a lender.

## Costs to weigh
FHA can cost more over time than a conventional loan with good credit, because of the insurance. It can still be the way to buy sooner. Model both in the [Real Estate Calculator](page:tools/real-estate/index.html#home) by switching the loan program.

## Other points
- The home must meet HUD property standards.
- Loan limits depend on the county.
- Some FHA rehabilitation loans exist. See [Renovation loans](resource:renovation-loans).
- Down-payment help can often be combined. See [Down-payment assistance](resource:down-payment-assistance).

## What a mortgage is
A mortgage is a loan secured by the home. You pay it back monthly over a term, commonly 15 or 30 years. Each payment covers **interest** and **principal**. Early on, most of it is interest. Your lender also usually collects **property tax and homeowners insurance** in the payment through an escrow account.

## The cost is more than the rate
- **Interest rate:** the yearly cost of borrowing.
- **APR:** the rate plus certain fees, expressed as a yearly percentage. It is a better way to compare offers.
- **Points:** an up-front fee that can lower the rate. Ask how long it takes to earn the cost back.
- **Closing costs:** lender, appraisal, title, recording and other fees, often a few percent of the price.
- **Mortgage insurance:** required on many loans with a small down payment. See each program page for how it works.

## Compare offers
Within a few days of applying, a lender must give you a **Loan Estimate**, a standard three-page form. Get one from at least three lenders on the same day with the same assumptions, then compare rate, APR, fees and cash to close line by line.

## What lenders look at
- **Credit scores and history.** See [Credit scores](resource:credit-scores).
- **Debt-to-income ratio (DTI):** your monthly debts, including the new payment, divided by your gross monthly income. Many lenders look for roughly 43% or less, but limits vary by program.
- **Down payment and cash reserves.**
- **Stable income and documentation.**
- **The property:** appraisal and condition.

## Prequalified or preapproved
Prequalification is a rough estimate. Preapproval means the lender has reviewed your documents and credit and states how much it would lend, subject to the property. Sellers take preapprovals more seriously.

## What it is
A timeshare gives you the right to use a resort or unit for a fixed week, a points allotment or a floating period. Ownership types differ: some give you a deed, others only a right to use. Read the contract to learn which you have.

## Compare with the alternatives
Price the lifetime cost: purchase price, financing interest, annual maintenance fees, special assessments, exchange fees and travel. Then compare with simply renting a place for the same weeks, or with a [vacation home](resource:vacation-home). Many people who plan to travel through retirement find renting more flexible.

## What to check before you buy
- **Contract type:** deeded, right-to-use or points. What happens at death? Can heirs refuse it?
- **Fees:** the current annual fee, how fast it has risen, and what the contract allows for increases.
- **Cancellation window:** many places give a short period to cancel after signing. Get the terms in writing before you pay.
- **Exit path:** ask what the developer or management company does if you want to give the timeshare back.
- **Sales pressure:** never buy on the day of a presentation without time to compare.

## Selling or exiting
The FTC warns that the resale market is crowded and that it may be hard or impossible to sell a timeshare. Warning signs:
- Unsolicited calls that claim a buyer is ready.
- Promises of a quick sale or guaranteed cancellation of the contract.
- Large upfront fees, or instructions to stop paying your mortgage or maintenance fees.

The FTC says to start with the resort's developer or management company and to check any reseller with your state attorney general. Prefer companies paid only after a sale, and get every promise and fee in writing.

## What it is
An accessory dwelling unit is a self-contained home on a lot that already has a main house. It can be detached, attached or converted from a garage or basement. It can house family, produce rent or both.

## Two gates before you spend
1. **Local zoning and permits.** Confirm the parcel allows an ADU, size limits, setbacks, parking, floodplain rules, occupancy approval and any restrictions on renting. Policies differ by locality. This guide makes no determination that any parcel permits an ADU.
2. **Financing.** Lenders treat projected rent differently. Ask whether they will count it and how construction is funded.

## What to price
Design, survey, engineering, permits, utility connections, site work, construction, contingency, interest during construction and the time to lease the unit.

## Delay test
Track months with no rent. Six extra months at **$1,400** a month of construction carrying cost consumes **$8,400** before the first permitted tenancy. Show the cash needed at the worst point as well as the stabilized annual cash flow.

## Use cases
- **Rent:** long-term tenants offset your own housing cost.
- **Family:** space for a parent or adult child. [Scarlet Ibis](animal:scarlet-ibis) compares housing, travel and care.
- **Aging in place:** a ground-floor unit can serve you later. [Swan](animal:swan) prices accessibility and transport.

## Insurance and taxes
Tell your insurer before construction: builder's risk and the transition to occupied coverage are separate questions. Rental income is taxable, and the unit has depreciation rules. See [Investment property](resource:investment-property).

## What it is
The **Federal Employees Retirement System (FERS)** covers most federal civilian employees hired in recent decades. It is a three-legged plan:
1. **The FERS basic benefit**, a lifetime pension based on your years of service and your highest average pay.
2. **Social Security**, because FERS employees pay into it.
3. **The Thrift Savings Plan (TSP)**, a 401(k)-style account with an agency match.

Use FERS in place of a New York ERS estimate if you work for the federal government. The pieces start at different times, so put each on its own timeline, as [Colossal Squid](animal:colossal-squid) does.

## Who can retire when (OPM)
- **Immediate retirement:** you may be entitled to a benefit that starts within 30 days of leaving if you meet the age and service requirements for your Minimum Retirement Age (MRA), which depends on your birth year.
- **MRA with 10 to 29 years:** the benefit is reduced by 5 percent for each year you are under 62, unless you have 20 years of service and your benefit starts at 60 or later.
- **Deferred retirement:** if you leave before meeting immediate-retirement rules, you may still qualify with at least 5 years of creditable civilian service, and you can receive benefits when you reach a qualifying age.
- **Early retirement:** available in certain involuntary separations, or voluntary separations during a major reorganization or reduction in force.
- **Disability:** a separate benefit with its own certification rules.

## TSP basics
The TSP uses the same 2026 employee limit as a 401(k): **$24,500**, with catch-up contributions for ages 50 and over. Agency contributions come on top. Withdrawals before 59½ may carry the additional 10% tax unless an exception applies, so check the rule that fits your separation date before planning around it.

## What to request
- A **retirement estimate** from your agency's HR or benefits office for each possible leave date.
- Your **Social Security earnings record** and an SSA estimate. See [Social Security](resource:social-security).
- Your **TSP balance** and projected withdrawal options.
- Rules for **health insurance in retirement** (FEHB), including how long you must have been enrolled before you retire.

> OPM, not a calculator, decides the final benefit. Treat any online estimate as a draft.

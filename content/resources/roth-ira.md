## What it is
A Roth IRA is an individual retirement account. You contribute money you have already paid tax on. Earnings grow tax-free, and qualified withdrawals are tax-free. There is no required withdrawal for the original owner.

## 2026 rules
- **Limit:** $7,500, or $8,600 if you are 50 or older, or your taxable compensation if less. The limit applies to all your traditional and Roth IRAs combined.
- **Income limits:** eligibility to contribute phases out between $153,000 and $168,000 for single filers and heads of household, and between $242,000 and $252,000 for married couples filing jointly.
- **Spousal IRA:** a married couple filing jointly may fund an IRA for a spouse with little or no income, based on the couple's combined taxable compensation. That is the idea behind [Rhesus Macaque Monkey](animal:rhesus-macaque-monkey).
- **No age limit** for making regular contributions as long as you have compensation.

## Why it is useful before retirement age
The IRS orders withdrawals in layers. Your **regular contributions** come out first and are not taxed or penalized, because you already paid tax. Next come **conversions**, then **earnings**. Earnings withdrawn before 59½ and before the account meets the five-year rule can be taxed and penalized. That layering is why [Green Anaconda](animal:green-anaconda) tracks contributions, conversions and earnings separately.

## Qualified withdrawals
A distribution is qualified, and so tax-free, when the account has been open at least five tax years and you are 59½ or older, disabled, or the distribution goes to a beneficiary after death. Up to a $10,000 lifetime amount for a first home can also qualify. Verify the details in Publication 590-B.

## Roth conversions
You can convert a traditional IRA or old pretax plan to a Roth and pay ordinary income tax on the amount converted. Each conversion has its own five-year clock for the early-withdrawal penalty. Convert only if you can pay the tax from other money.

## Keep records
Record every contribution and conversion by year, and keep Form 5498 and Form 1099-R. If you ever need to prove basis, the brokerage's year-end statements may not show it.

## The two dates
- **Statement closing date:** the day the issuer totals your balance. That balance is usually what gets reported to the bureaus.
- **Payment due date:** the day by which you must pay at least the minimum to avoid a late fee and a late-payment mark. Paying the full statement balance by this date generally means no interest on new purchases.

Paying before the statement closes can lower the balance that gets reported, even if you pay in full later.

## Utilization
Utilization is your balance divided by your credit limit, on each card and overall. It sits inside the "amounts owed" part of a FICO score, about 30% of the total. Lower is generally better. Many lenders view under 30% well. There is no official cut-off, and some people with top scores use far less.

## Interest and fees
- **APR** is the yearly interest rate. A balance carried past the due date accrues interest, so a card at 22% costs roughly 1.8% a month.
- **Minimum payments** cover little more than interest. Paying only the minimum on a large balance can take years.
- **Annual fees** are worth paying only if rewards and benefits exceed them.

## Building credit with a card
1. Put one small recurring bill on the card.
2. Set autopay for the full balance.
3. Keep utilization low and avoid applying for several cards at once.
4. Let the account age. The age of accounts counts for about 15% of a FICO score.

## Do not do this
Do not carry a balance to earn rewards. Do not use a card for spending you could not pay off. Do not give card numbers to anyone you did not contact first.

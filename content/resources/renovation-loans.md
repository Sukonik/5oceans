## The idea
A standard mortgage is based on the home as it is. A renovation loan is based on the value after the work, and the repair money is held in escrow and paid out as work is completed. That makes it possible to buy a home that needs updating and finance the repairs at mortgage rates.

## Main options
- **FHA 203(k):** insured by HUD. It generally targets eligible owner-occupied existing homes. A lender in the program and a licensed contractor are required. HUD's page describes the limited and standard versions.
- **Fannie Mae HomeStyle:** a conventional loan that can finance renovations on a primary home, second home or investment property, with its own rules.
- **USDA Section 504:** repair loans and grants for eligible low-income owner-occupants in rural areas. Grants are for owners aged 62 or older.

## What to expect
- **Bids and plans:** you need written contractor bids and a scope of work.
- **Draws:** funds are released in stages after inspections.
- **Contingency:** lenders often require a reserve for surprises.
- **Time:** these loans take longer to close than a standard mortgage.
- **Occupancy:** a dedicated rental usually needs a different loan. See [Investment property](resource:investment-property).

## Compare with the alternatives
- Paying cash for repairs after closing.
- A [home-equity line](resource:home-equity-and-reverse) once you own the home.
- A [construction-to-permanent loan](resource:construction-and-land-loans) when you build new.

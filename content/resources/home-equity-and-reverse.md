## Home equity loan
A one-time loan, often called a second mortgage, with a fixed rate and a set monthly payment. It suits a known expense such as a renovation.

## Home equity line of credit (HELOC)
A revolving line of credit you can draw from during a draw period, with interest-only or flexible payments, then repay. Rates are usually variable, so the payment can rise.

## Cash-out refinance
You replace your mortgage with a larger one and take the difference in cash. You pay closing costs again, and you may lose a lower rate on the old loan.

## Reverse mortgage (HECM)
The federally insured Home Equity Conversion Mortgage lets an owner aged 62 or older borrow against the home without monthly mortgage payments. The borrower must:
- own the home and live in it as a primary residence,
- keep paying property taxes, insurance and upkeep,
- complete **HUD-approved counseling** before the loan.

The balance grows with interest and is repaid when the last borrower sells, moves out or dies. It reduces what is left for heirs, so compare it with alternatives first. See [Aging in place](animal:swan).

## Risks common to all of these
- The home is collateral. If you cannot pay, you can lose it.
- Variable rates can rise.
- Fees and closing costs add up.
- Borrowing against equity to cover everyday spending can leave you with less later.

## Compare before you borrow
Consider savings, a smaller home, [renting out a unit](resource:adu), or [selling and right-sizing](resource:primary-home). Talk to a [HUD-approved counselor](resource:housing-counseling) first.

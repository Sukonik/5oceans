## What it is
Buying land means purchasing an empty or lightly improved lot, either to build on, hold or rent. Building a rental house on purchased land is the longest, most uncertain route in this shelf. [Hermit Crab](animal:hermit-crab) includes it as one option.

## Before you buy
- **Zoning and use.** Does the zoning allow the home you want, and any rental or accessory unit?
- **Access and utilities.** Legal road access, water, power, and sewer or a septic system. Ask about connection fees.
- **Septic and soil.** If there is no sewer, check septic requirements and site suitability with the local health department. EPA's septic pages explain the basics.
- **Flood and wetlands.** Check the FEMA flood map and local wetland or coastal rules. Flood zones can limit building and raise insurance costs.
- **Survey and title.** Confirm boundaries, easements and ownership.
- **Taxes and carrying cost.** Land has property tax and no income, and land is not depreciable.

## What to price
Land, closing costs, utility connections, design, permits, construction, contingency, loan draws, interest during construction, inspections, conversion to permanent financing and time until the first tenant.

## Financing
Lenders may require larger down payments for land than for a finished home, and rules differ for a construction-to-permanent loan. Get written terms from more than one lender, and use zero rent during design and construction in your cash-flow model.

## Delay and cost risk
Plan for permit delays and overruns. A short delay can cost thousands in carrying cost. Compare with buying a finished home or an [ADU](resource:adu) on land you already own.
